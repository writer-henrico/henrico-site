In my world, I never expected a relative to die so young, especially without knowing why. And yet, right then, I was on my way to a funeral.

Inside a black car with tinted windows, I was sitting in the back seat, adjusting the black dress I’d thrown on in a hurry.

“So, what happened to my mother?” I asked the burly ghoul from our family who was driving.

Unfortunately, I didn’t get an answer.

“Are you ignoring me because you don’t know anything? Or do you know and are hiding it from me?”

And once again, silence.

Which probably meant he was hiding the truth from me.

But a ghoul can’t lie to us.

Just as I was about to give the command, he interrupted me.

“I’m pretty sure you’ll find out as soon as you get there.”

*What a pain.*

I looked into the distance in the late afternoon. I could barely see anything outside.

“All right, I’ll wait then.”

---

I arrived at the mansion.

I couldn’t explain how I felt.

But the place certainly didn’t have the atmosphere of a funeral.

Everyone was wearing formal attire and looked serious.

They were talking as if they were discussing numbers.

*Was I at a business meeting?*

When I got closer, the conversations stopped, and everyone turned to look at me.

I took a few more steps and felt as if their eyes were following me.

An unimaginable pressure.

Even though I was part of the family and the Umbra clan, it was as if I were an outsider.

“Hanna!” A woman’s voice called out to me.

My mind snapped back to reality the moment I heard that voice.

“Aunt Lisa!”

She was wearing a plain black dress with lace on the sleeves and hem that matched her curly black hair.

I turned my attention to my clothes. My dress had a mullet hem; I was wearing black tights, knee-high boots, and lace gloves.

Clothes that looked like something a goth teenager would wear.

I felt a little self-conscious.

*Although vampires are kind of goth, aren’t they? So I guess I’m fine.*

“How are you doing?” she asked as she hugged me. “I know that was sudden.”

I looked into the distance.

My eyes fell upon a flower garden near the entrance, beneath the large stained-glass window. There stood the black lilies, their cup-shaped petals seeming to spill the darkness into the night.

My mom loved those flowers. They looked so fresh that I could have sworn she had watered them that morning.

If they were there, then maybe my mom could be there too.

“It doesn’t seem real,” I said, my eyes filling with tears.

Everything’s going to be all right,” Aunt Lisa said, hugging me a little tighter.

“Hanna, your mother asked me for a favor before she left,” she whispered.

“What?”

Her voice grew even quieter. I could barely hear her. But I felt as if she were speaking directly to my soul.

“Whatever happens today…. Don’t drink your mother’s blood.”

My heart started beating faster.

The mansion door opened.

My aunt held me tightly.

A dark figure appeared.

She wore a voluminous burgundy dress with black accents that emphasized her pear-shaped silhouette. Her face was covered by a veil.

“I see that everyone has arrived. You may come in.”

I recognized that voice.

It was Carmen, my great-great-great-grandmother, the leader of our clan.

I felt as though she were crushing me in her arms.

“Aunt, you’re hurting me.”

“Sorry.”

I struggled to break free from her arms, and she slowly let go of me.

Aunt Lisa was gripping her dress tightly. Her eyes were fixed on Carmen, who was standing by the door.

“I didn’t understand what you said before, Aunt.”

She looked at me again and smiled.

“Let’s go inside.”

We followed the others into the mansion.

---

The large dining table was set. The silverware and serving dishes reflected the light from the chandeliers.

The main dishes included herb-roasted chicken, lasagna, and homemade bread.

There were fine wines and glasses of blood.

There was also a large cherry cake for dessert.

I turned my attention to the glasses of blood.

*Don’t drink your mother’s blood,* I thought, remembering my aunt’s words.

At least the blood came from reliable suppliers. Or so they said.

*I wonder if they tampered with it?*

A vampire has to feed, so I decided to take the risk.

As soon as I drank, I felt stronger.

*A natural reaction. No big deal.*

My mother was in the other room, far away from here. The blood in the glasses had nothing to do with hers.

*Why would I do that, Auntie? It doesn’t make sense.*

I looked around at the guests again.

*Wasn’t anyone sad?*

The sound of cutlery and conversation, accompanied by laughter.

I felt isolated.

The marks on everyone’s hands haunted me.

I looked down at my own hands. No marks.

Everyone there had them.

My aunt and my mom did, too.

I remembered a conversation.

*What are those marks?*

*They’re birthmarks,* my mom had replied.

I was the only one who wasn’t born with them.

My aunt Lisa came closer.

She wasn’t like the others. I could tell.

“Hanna, you haven’t gone to see your mom yet. Are you afraid?” she asked, a look of concern on her face.

I hugged my aunt.

“Yes,” I replied, swallowing hard.

She gently stroked my head.

“It’s okay. I’ll go with you. Everything will be fine.”

She gave me one last gentle pat before letting go. Then I headed toward the hall where my mother was.

It was a large, spacious room with a stained-glass window at the back.

The coffin stood in front of it.

Several wooden chairs were arranged in rows.

A red carpet ran between them.

Aunt Lisa took my hand and slowly led me forward.

When I got closer, she let go of my hand, and I saw my mother lying there.

She was smiling.

It was as if she were sleeping.

Tears fell.

A deep sadness settled over me.

*I wonder what she was thinking before she died?*

*If she could say anything to me, what would she say?*

Carmen entered the room.

“Please take your seats. Let’s begin the ceremony.”

---

All the guests were seated.

Red candles were lit.

One by one, the guests stood up, spoke, and then sat down again.

However, the words that came out of their mouths were not words of sadness or longing.

They were words of anger.

They were words of hatred.

Words of vengeance.

*We have to kill the Vespers.*

“Hanna, come forward,” Carmen ordered.

Everyone turned to look at me.

They were waiting.

I looked around, confused.

I stood up slowly.

I walked down the red carpet until I reached Carmen’s side.

The eyes of everyone present followed my every move.

Carmen ordered two items to be brought forward.

Two ghouls dressed as butlers approached, wearing gloves and carrying the items with the utmost care.

A crystal goblet and a silver knife.

With the knife, Carmen cut into my mother’s heart and extracted her blood using her powers.

She poured the blood into the goblet.

“Even though Seraphine has died, we have not. Today, we will have a new soldier in our army. Someone young and full of energy who can help us exterminate the Vespers.”

She offered me the goblet and continued.

“Now, Hanna, drink your mother’s blood.”

I looked at her, unsure.

“Wait. This is all happening too fast. I didn’t think I’d be drinking my mother’s blood.”

“You have to drink it. It’s part of the ritual.”

“But I need answers. Why did my mother die? Who killed her? Who are our enemies?”

Even though she was wearing a veil, Carmen’s heavy sigh made her impatience clear.

“I don’t need to explain anything to you. As soon as you drink it, all of her final memories will be passed on to you.”

She offered me the goblet again.

I took the goblet.

I gently swirled it, watching the liquid sway from side to side.

If I drank it, I’d have the answers I wanted.

I would know what my mother had been thinking.

I would see her one more time, even if only through her memories.

I could just drink it and deal with the consequences later.

Slowly, I brought the goblet to my lips.

When I glanced at my aunt, she looked apprehensive.

*Don’t drink your mother’s blood.*

That’s what she had said.

That’s what my mother had asked her to do.

My hand began to shake.

*What did you mean by that?*

I lowered the goblet.

I looked at the coffin, searching for answers.

*Why shouldn’t I drink?*

I took my mother’s hand.

I didn’t know why I’d done that. I think it was instinctive.

Her birthmark was gone.

Something was wrong.

I slowly placed the goblet on the edge of the coffin.

“No. I’m not going to drink. Not until you explain it to me.”

The guests began to murmur.

“You’ll get your explanations. Just drink.”

“Just explain it to me, and I’ll drink.”

Carmen clicked her tongue in disapproval and waved her hand.

A few ghouls appeared.

I lost sight of Aunt Lisa.

“That’s what your mother wanted. It’s part of the tradition. Don’t you want to be part of this family?”

“That’s a lie. Why are you lying to me?”

Some guests stood up.

Carmen motioned with her hand, and they sat back down.

The ghouls were closing in.

I kept moving farther and farther toward the back of the room.

Then I sensed them move.

Just then, my aunt jumped in front of me.

She was holding a bloodstained spear.

“Run. I’ll hold them off.”

I looked all around for a way out.

But I was surrounded.

My only option was what was behind me.

The stained-glass window.

I covered my face with my arms and threw myself at the window.

Shards of glass flew everywhere.

I landed among the black lilies.

I forced myself to move, then sprinted toward the forest.
