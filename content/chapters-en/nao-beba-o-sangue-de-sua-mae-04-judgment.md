There were vampires everywhere.

Some had gone inside the cabin and were turning it upside down.

I didn’t know how much longer we’d be staying there.

And I didn’t know what we were waiting for.

A mist began to appear.

“He’s coming.”

Everyone present was on edge.

From afar, among the trees, I saw a figure in dark clothes walking slowly toward us.

“Try not to anger him,” Adrian whispered.

A man with slicked-back gray hair approached. He was wearing a black robe over a dark suit, which contrasted with his red tie.

When he stopped in front of us, it felt as though a massive wall had suddenly risen before me.

Maybe it was because I was on my knees, but he seemed even taller from that position.

“Adrian, you traitor,” he said in a raspy voice.

“Save your insults, Severino. If you want to take it out on me, you have every right. But let her go.”

“That’s where you’re wrong! She has everything to do with this.”

The vampires inside the cabin rushed out to meet the elder.

“Lord Severino, we searched everywhere and found nothing inside the cabin.”

Adrian let out a challenging smile.

“See?”

*The fireplace?*

Severino, on the other hand, remained serious, his expression unchanged. “Aren’t you forgetting something?”

Adrian looked worried.

“I have nothing to hide.”

Severino pointed at me.

“The girl.”

Adrian’s tone turned cold. His nervousness was evident.

“She’s proof of your betrayal. Hiding someone from the Umbra clan makes you guilty.”

“Lord Severino, you’ve misunderstood.”

“Bring the spear.”

The vampires brought a red cloth with something wrapped inside.

Severino unfolded the cloth, revealing a large, gleaming silver spear, and pointed the weapon at Adrian.

My heart raced.

I couldn’t let this happen.

I jumped in front of him.

“Don’t do this. He wasn’t hiding me! I’m right here. If you want to kill me, go ahead and kill me.”

Severino was completely baffled by my reaction.

“Why are you defending him? You know the Umbra clan will be disappointed in you.”

“I don’t care what my clan thinks. He saved my life. I owe him, and that’s all that matters.”

Severino was dumbfounded.

“What’s your name?”

“Hanna.”

“Are you Seraphine’s daughter?”

“Yes. Apparently, you know my mother, too. That doesn’t impress me.”

He burst out laughing.

“Do you know who this man is?”

“Yes, of course.”

“Are you sure? You just met him. Do you really think you know him?”

He was right, after all. I had just met him, and he seemed to be hiding more than he let on.

My heart filled with doubts.

Severino continued, smiling grimly.

“No, you don’t know him. So, let me explain something to you, little girl. He’s the man who killed your mother.”

I felt a tightness in my chest.

A knot in my throat.

I’d been deceived.

“No, that’s a lie! He wouldn’t do that!”

“Look at him. You’ll see who he really is.”

I looked at Adrian, searching for a denial, for some sign of anger that would show he thought Severino was lying.

His eyes filled with tears, and he looked away.

“No!” I screamed and started crying.

“Let him go,” Severino ordered.

Two vampires approached him and removed his handcuffs.

“Adrian, it seems, is a very kind man. He didn’t want to kill you because you’re just a child, but you’re still an Umbra.”

Severino placed his palm in front of his face and his mark began to glow.

“For the injustice suffered by the Vesper clan, I invoke the oath. We will fight forever. We won’t stop until the Umbras are eradicated from this land.”

“Adrian! Kill that damned Umbra.”

Severino pointed at him, and he stood up.

The man I had once admired for his size and strong muscles.

Now he was terrifying.

Murderer.

This is the real Adrian.

I would be killed just like my mother.

He raised his fist high.

I closed my eyes.

I heard the impact of the blow and felt the wind brush past my face.

For a moment, I thought he’d hit me. But when I opened my eyes, my handcuffs were broken.

Adrian had struck the metal without hitting me.

“What are you doing? You were supposed to kill her! You really are a traitor to the very end!”

Adrian held up his hand to show them the mark. It wasn’t glowing.

“Your order didn’t work.”

Everyone was startled.

Adrian took my hand and turned his back on the old man.

Severino slowly composed himself, his expression settling back into the same stern look as before.

“I see. So she didn’t make the blood pact. Not even after her own mother’s death.”

One of the vampires beside him seemed to hesitate.

“Sir, what are we going to do?”

Severino shot him an irritated glance.

“Nothing. The Vampire Court’s treaty doesn’t allow us to punish her for belonging to a clan. Without the blood pact, she’s free.”

Adrian smiled with relief.

“You’ve proven your loyalty this time, Adrian. But we’ll see how long that lasts.”

Severino chuckled softly, then turned away and signaled to the other vampires. One by one, they began to leave.

Adrian approached me.

I stepped back and held up a hand to keep him away.

“Don’t come near me!”

“Hanna, I can explain.”

My phone started beeping. A message had come in with a single photo attached. It showed my aunt Lisa, bound and wounded on an altar.

The message was clear.

Either I came back, or she would die.

I looked at the photo one more time.

I couldn’t trust Adrian. Not after what I’d just found out. If I went back, I’d have to face everything alone.

With difficulty, I typed the mansion’s address into the GPS. I swallowed my tears and wiped my face.

“Don’t go,” Adrian begged.

“What does that have to do with you?”

“I…”

“You killed my mother! You kept this from me all this time! You traitor!”

He fell silent.

I turned and ran toward my destination.

“Your mother wouldn’t want you to go!” he shouted after me.

“She’ll understand,” I whispered. I wasn’t answering Adrian. I was apologizing to my mother.
