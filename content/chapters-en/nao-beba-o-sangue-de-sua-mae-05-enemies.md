I was gasping for breath, covered in cuts. My arms hurt, and my legs could barely hold me up.

There were so many vampires in front of me that I lost count.

I managed to subdue one, but two others charged at me. For a moment, I thought I might win. Then more vampires joined the fight, and my attacks stopped having any effect.

There was no way I could defeat them.

The worst part was realizing that they weren’t trying to kill me. They’d back off when I attacked, surround me, and then advance again, as if they were having fun.

Carmen watched it all with a smile.

That’s when I understood. She wasn’t trying to defeat me. She was testing me, pleased to have found a potential replacement for her army.

I wasn’t a person to her. I was a tool.

And if I couldn’t defeat her, all I could do was threaten what she valued most.

I pressed the knife to my throat.

Carmen immediately ordered everyone to stop, and they obeyed.

“Would you rather die than be part of this family?”

I looked at Carmen.

“I want to make a deal.”

“And what would that be?”

“Let Aunt Lisa go. I want you to forgive her for what she did, and then I’ll drink my mother’s blood.”

“She committed a crime. She rebelled against the clan. Why should I do that?”

“We’ve already lost my mother. We can’t lose anyone else. I think you can forgive her.”

Carmen was silent for a few moments.

Then she gave an order.

Shortly afterward, they brought Aunt Lisa in. Another ghoul appeared carrying the ceremonial crystal goblet. Carmen handed it to my aunt.

“Give her the goblet. If you do that, I’ll forgive your betrayal.”

Lisa held the goblet but didn’t move right away. When she approached me, her eyes were brimming with tears.

“Don’t do this, Hanna.”

“I don’t want to lose you too, Auntie. I’ve already lost my mother.”

I took the goblet from her hands and stared at the red liquid.

“Forgive me, Aunt. Forgive me, Mom.”

I raised the goblet to my lips and drank the blood.

My head began to ache.

The world spun, and voices flooded my mind. I felt as if my very being were shattering.

Everything went dark.

For a moment, I smelled black lilies, even though I couldn’t see them.

When I could see again, I was standing in front of the mansion, near the black lilies.

Lisa was standing in front of me.

“Lih, I’m going to do something crazy. I need to ask you for a favor.”

“What?”

“I’m going to break into the sacred forest. If anything happens to me, I want you to take care of Hanna.”

Lisa frowned, indignant.

“Have you lost your mind? You could die!”

“Maybe there’s a way to break the curse of the mark in that forest. I don’t want to keep fighting for something I didn’t choose. I want to be free.”

I took her hands in mine.

“Promise me you won’t let Hanna drink my blood when I die.”

“But that’s impossible!”

“Do whatever it takes. I don’t want her to suffer the same fate as me.”

My mind went blank again.

Other images flashed before my eyes.

I was in the sacred forest, surrounded by werewolves. They were enormous humanoid creatures, their claws slicing through the air with every strike.

My speed and strength were superior to theirs. I created swords of blood and managed to take down several of them, but the attacks didn’t stop. Little by little, I began to run out of breath.

One of the werewolves lunged at me when I was no longer able to dodge.

Adrian leaped in front of me and took the blow in my place.

Even though he was wounded, he joined me and fought the werewolves. His movements were slower than usual.

When they retreated, Adrian collapsed to the ground.

I ran to him and crouched down beside him.

“Sera, you idiot!”

“Be quiet.”

He pressed his hand against the wound, trying to contain the blood.

“You need to run. They’ll be back with reinforcements.”

“I can’t give up now.”

“I won’t be able to help you if you stay here.”

I placed my hand on his face.

“It’s okay. This fight is mine.”

Adrian didn’t answer. He just stared at me, as if he wanted to say something but couldn’t find the words.

My mark glowed.

I stood up, startled.

“Why now?”

Then I heard the command.

The voice inside my head was unmistakable.

“Kill the Vespers.”

I shook my head, trying to push those words away.

No. I wouldn’t do that.

But the more I resisted, the more the pain intensified.

Adrian stood up too. Even wounded, he summoned a blood claw.

I knew what would happen if we fought. I was stronger than him at that moment. If I obeyed the mark, he would die.

I raised my sword.

Blood swirled around us, ready to strike.

But my body no longer obeyed me.

Stop.

I tried to stop the blow before it even happened.

Stop.

Pain shot through my body.

Stop!

Adrian lunged forward.

For a split second, I had a chance to defend myself.

I didn’t.

The blood-soaked claw pierced my chest.

I felt no pain. For the first time, the voice inside my head was silent.

I felt relief.

Blood trickled from my mouth, and the marks on our hands stopped glowing.

I fell to the ground.

Adrian knelt beside me.

“Sera! Why didn’t you fight back?”

I smiled, despite the pain.

“I managed to resist the mark. I’m free now.”

He gently brushed his hand over my face. His tears trickled into my eyes.

“Sera… Please…”

I tried to answer, but breathing was already difficult.

“Stay still. Don’t push yourself,” he begged.

I needed to say it.

The words wouldn’t come out.

I needed Hanna to know.

“Tell… Hanna… I love… her.”

I smiled one last time.

Adrian’s face grew more and more distant. His voice faded until everything went dark.

I opened my eyes.

I was back in the present.

For a few moments, I couldn’t do anything but cry.

I looked at my hand.

A mark was starting to appear on my skin.

Now I understood.

My mother had tried to escape that curse to protect me, but I had inherited the same prison.

Lisa came over and hugged me.

I collapsed into her arms, crying harder than I ever had before.

---

Days later, I was still thinking about my mother’s memories.

I decided to look for Adrian. I finally understood what had happened, though I didn’t know how to face him.

I found him near the border between our territories.

“You’d better not cross this line,” he warned.

When I saw him, I felt a violent urge. I wanted to kill him.

I clenched my fists and forced myself to stand still.

Adrian noticed my reaction.

“You’ve become a Bloodbound…”

“How do you know?”

“You feel it too, don’t you? That hostility.”

I hesitated. He was right.

“I do.”

Adrian looked away.

“I’d better go. I don’t want to risk killing you.”

He turned to leave.

“See you later, Hanna.”

He hesitated.

“Now we’re enemies.”
